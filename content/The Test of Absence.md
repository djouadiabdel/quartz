title: The Test of Absence
---

*Case File 04. The absence.*

The strongest forensic move is not about what a document says. It is about the document that should exist and does not.

Investigators use this constantly and it is more powerful than positive evidence, because a forger controls what he writes and cannot control what the world contains. If a man tells you he spent the evening at a restaurant, the useful question is rarely whether his account is internally consistent. It is whether the receipt exists, whether the staff remember him, whether the traffic camera has him on the road. The gap is the finding. A story that leaves the wrong hole behind it is a story that did not happen the way it was told.

Applied to texts, the move is this. Suppose the ordinary thing happened. Work out what the surviving record would have to look like. Then go see whether that record exists.

## Run the test

So suppose the cover-up worked. Suppose the account we have is what it would have been if the machinery had run to completion the way it does everywhere else.

What would we be reading?

## The version that should exist

It is not hard to reconstruct, because we have hundreds of examples of the genre and it is highly regular.

A genuine blasphemer, justly dealt with. The charge would be stated as fact, not as an accusation, and the text would establish the guilt early and firmly, the way Oedipus establishes it, because the guilt is what converts a killing into an act of order.

The authorities would come out competent and reluctant. Not villains. Men who saw the danger, weighed it, and did the necessary thing at cost to themselves.

The crowd would be right. Frightened, perhaps ugly in the moment, but correct about the essential matter.

The unrest would end. The city would return to peace, and the account would credit the execution with the return.

And then, at a decent distance, the man would quietly become a god. Or a hero, or a warning, or a shrine on the site. This is the standard ending. The victim gets divinized after the fact, precisely because the killing worked, and his new sanctity is the community's way of remembering that it was healed without remembering how.

That account would have been easy to write, easy to keep, and useful to everyone who kept it. It fits the culture, the genre, and the incentives perfectly.

## We have none of it

Not a fragment. Not a competing early version that got suppressed and left traces in the polemics. Not a strand inside the surviving text where you can see the tidy account showing through underneath.

The charge is presented as false by the document itself. The authorities are incompetent and afraid. The crowd is wrong. The peace does not arrive. And the divinization, the one element that is present, arrives in the wrong order and with the wrong content, attached to the man as he was before the killing rather than conferred on him afterward as compensation.

What survives is the mirror image of the expected document, point for point.

## Why the absence is the finding

I want to be careful about the form of this argument, because arguments from silence are usually weak and this one has to earn its keep.

An argument from silence is weak when the missing thing had no particular reason to exist. It is strong when the missing thing had every reason to exist, when the conditions for producing it were ideal, and when it is still missing.

Here the conditions were ideal. The culture had the genre. It had the motive. It had the authority and the centuries. It had, in the reconstruction above, a version that was easier to defend, less embarrassing to its keepers, and more useful to everyone involved.

That version never formed.

## The question

A hole in the record is not nothing. A hole with edges, in exactly the shape of the document that should be sitting in it, is evidence in its own right.

A gap shaped exactly like a fingerprint. What made it?
