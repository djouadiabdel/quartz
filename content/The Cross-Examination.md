---
title: The Cross-Examination
---

There are two men in my head and they have never once agreed on anything.

The first one I call Voss. He has read everything, and he has an answer for everything, and the answer is always clean, and it is always, underneath, the same answer. I will let him tell you what it is. He wins every round. What eats him alive is that the rest of us will not just say so.

The other one I call Ames. Ames has almost nothing. One thread and two tired hands. He loses every round, and he knows he loses, and he will not get up from the table.

I set a verse down in front of the two of them and let them go. Eight of them, the smallest seed to the empty grave. I did not step in. I would not know where to start.

> It is like a mustard seed, which is the smallest of all seeds on earth.
> Mark 4:31

**Voss.** Here. Here is where it went, and I want you to sit in it with me, because the size of it is the whole thing, the size is the part nobody will look at.

Smallest of all seeds. It is not. It is not even close. Poppy is smaller. Orchid is smaller. A child with a hand lens could stand up in the back of the room and correct the Son of God before the sentence was cold. And do not tell me parable. Do not tell me genre. I wrote the lectures on genre. I know what a parable is and I know what a mistake is, and this is a man saying a false thing about the world because he did not know the true thing.

And it is tiny, yes, it is nothing, who cares, a seed, and that is EXACTLY where they lose me, because I am the one who cares, I was built to care, and once you catch the machine in one lie you have caught the machine. One. That is all it takes. A thing that says every syllable fell out of the mouth of God does not get one.

And it is never just the seed, that is what keeps me up. The girl dying in Mark and already dead in Matthew. The day he died, which John and Mark cannot line up, cannot, I have laid the columns side by side a hundred times. And I can explain every one of them, that is the thing I cannot get a single soul to feel. I can hold the whole broken pile in one head at one time and hand you the cause of every crack. Men. Men wrote it apart from each other and the seams show because there was nobody behind them smoothing the seams.

And a real God, an actual one, did not survive me. ME. A man who dies, who forgets where he put his keys. I sat down with a lexicon and the thing came apart in my hands, and a real God does not get audited by a graduate student and fail. Saying otherwise is not humility, it is cowardice. It is finished. I closed it forty years ago and it has not made a sound since, and I am so tired of being told I am the one who lost something.

**Ames.** I am not going to take the seed from you. Keep it. You are right that it is wrong, right that it is small, right that the man who taught you genre cannot make the smallness go away by naming it. Keep all of it.

I only want the next four sentences. The ones your eye always slides off. Because in the same breath, before the parables are even finished, Matthew stops and tells you flat out why this man talks in seeds and fields and buried things at all. So was fulfilled what was spoken through the prophet, he says. I will utter things hidden since the foundation of the world.

Now sit in that the way you just sat in the seed. Hidden since the foundation of the world. Not since last Tuesday. Since the founding. And this is the part I want you of all people to reason out with me, because you are the man who digs, you are the one who finds the buried hand in the text.

Every city in the old world has a founding story, and when you scratch the founding story you find a body under it. Rome gets founded and the first thing that happens is Romulus kills Remus, brother over the wall, and they lay the bricks anyway. Go further back, go to the first city in your own Bible, and who builds it. Cain. Cain builds the first city four verses after he kills his brother in a field, and God tells him the blood is screaming up out of the ground, and Cain's answer is to go pour a town over it and name the streets after his son.

That is not a coincidence you get to file, Voss. That is a pattern and it is everywhere, it is Thebes, it is every people that ever cut a throat to bring the rain and then carved the throat-cutting into the temple wall and called it the will of the gods.

That is the thing hidden since the foundation of the world. A killing. The one the whole town agreed on. And you want to know why it stays hidden, you, the man who lives for the buried thing. It stays hidden because the people who tell the story are the people who did the killing, and no killer tells it straight. The story is the alibi. In the story the dead man is always guilty. He brought the plague. He broke the taboo. He had it coming. Every myth on earth is the crowd's version, written by the ones holding the rocks, and in the crowd's version the town sleeps fine.

And then, once, in one book, the story gets told from under the pile. From the dirt. The victim opens his mouth in the crowd's own book and says they hated me without a reason, and it stops reading like a myth, because for the first time it is the man on the ground talking and not the men with the stones.

That is the harvest. That is what the seed was buried over. You stood in a field that was planted, on purpose, right on top of the oldest grave in the human race, and the text put a shovel in your hand and said here, this is the thing I came to dig up, and you got down on your knees and measured the seed and went home and told everyone the farmer was bad at botany.

> Let any one of you who is without sin be the first to throw a stone at her.
> John 8:7

**Voss.** Take the one you love. Go on, put it down in front of me, because I can shut this one too and I want you to watch me do it without my pulse changing.

The woman, the stones, he stoops and writes in the dirt, let the one without sin throw first, and the whole mob drains into the street, oldest to youngest. The most human thing in the book. And it is not in the oldest manuscripts. It is not in the earliest John. A scribe slid it in three hundred years after the fact.

And I do not believe that, Ames, I KNOW it, cold, the way I know the back of my own hand, because finding the exact seam where a later hand got in is the thing I did with my one and only life, forty years of it, squinting at ink under glass. So the most beautiful scene in the Gospels is a forgery. A splice. Somebody else's hand wearing the man's voice.

And here is what I want you to actually look at. Not the manuscript. Me. You hand me the loveliest thing you own and I already have the drawer open, I do not even reach, later insertion, in it goes, the drawer shuts, and I feel NOTHING. Nothing. Clean. And that, that I can do it and feel nothing, is the thing I would stand up in front of God and the whole faculty and defend to the death and call it honesty.

**Ames.** You are right about the manuscript. I will not touch you on the text, you would take my hand off, you know it in your sleep. So leave the text. Stay on the last thing you said, the part you did not know you were confessing.

You feel nothing when the drawer shuts. You said it like a medal. So think about what a man is who can file the most beautiful thing he has ever read and feel nothing at all. That is not a man standing free in a field. That is a man in a very clean, very small, very well lit room who has stopped trying the door, because the door is painted on the wall, and the terrible part, the part you cannot see from inside, is that he is the one who painted it. Every drawer that shuts that cleanly is one more coat on the paint.

But leave that too. Here is the thing your drawer could not hold, and you should lose sleep over it. The oldest copies did not have this scene. And the church could not keep it out. For a thousand years the scribes kept smuggling it back in against their own manuscripts, because it was too true to lose.

True about what. Watch a stoning happen, slowly, the way it actually happens. A crowd with rocks and one accused in the middle, and here is the thing nobody tells you, nobody wants to throw first. Everybody stands there with the stone going heavy in his hand, waiting, because the first stone is the one that makes it a mob, and the second the first one flies, nobody is guilty anymore, because everybody is. That is the whole engine of it. One accused, all of them, and the terrible peace that comes down on a town the moment it has agreed on the one it gets to throw.

And one man kneels in the dirt and will not hand them the first stone. He gives each of them his own face back instead. And the machine that needed all of them moving as one just comes apart in the road, the old men leaving first, because the old men have thrown the most stones and have the most faces to answer for.

You have a drawer for the verse. You never built one for the fact that your own hand, right now, will not close around a rock. Somebody pried that hand open a long time before you were born. You spend your life finding the hand that slipped into a manuscript. You have never once gone looking for the one that got into yours.

> Jesus reached out his hand and touched the man. "I am willing," he said. "Be clean!"
> Mark 1:41

**Voss.** He touches a leper. Lays his bare hand on the one body in the country that not the priest, not the mother who bore him, will go within ten feet of. Lovely. And I can tell you what it is, all the way to the floor, because I have the name and the name shuts the door behind it.

Compassion. Evolved. It is in the chimps, Ames, go watch the chimps. It is a herd animal keeping its herd alive, and yes, YES, it feels like heaven from the inside, do not tell me it does not, I felt it flood me on my knees for twenty years, but from the outside it is a chemical and three hundred thousand years of apes who happened to live because they huddled.

And here is the part that should end the conversation and does not, because people will not be reasonable. I am kinder now, off my knees, godless, than I ever was praying. I got here on a hormone. There is a cause for the kindness and the cause is an animal, and the day you are finally holding the animal you can set the miracle down in the road and walk off, and I did, and I do not miss it, because the animal does every last thing the miracle promised and it never once asks me to believe a thing I cannot put under glass.

**Ames.** Some of it is the animal. Not this. And I am going to use your Darwin on you, because you left it lying right there.

The animal does not walk toward the sick one. The animal cannot afford to. The herd stays alive by doing the exact opposite of this, by driving the sick one out, by shouldering the weak and the marked and the catching one to the edge of the group and letting the cold have it, so the rest stay clean. That is not a Bible verse, that is your own book. Run the tape on any troop of your beloved chimps. The wounded one goes to the margin and dies alone. That is the wiring. That is three hundred thousand years of it.

And the human herd did it with its whole heart and a clear conscience. Your Greeks, the ones you read at dawn now, carried the wrong-shaped newborn out to the hillside at night and set it down in the frost and walked home and slept like men who had done a chore. Sparta kept a cliff for it. Every people on the map had its lepers outside the wall, its untouchable caste, its one marked wretch it could drive out so the rest of the village could breathe. That is not cruelty breaking the rule. That is the rule. That is the animal you say you are holding, doing precisely what it was built to do.

So when a man walks the other way, walks straight at the leper and puts a bare hand on him, that is not the old wiring firing warm. That is the old wiring thrown hard into reverse. It was not obvious. It was not even sane, by every rule that stood before it. It only became obvious, it only became the floor under your feet that you now call basic decency, because enough people watched one man do the insane thing on purpose, over and over, the leper, the tax man, the whore at his own table, until the floor of the world quietly moved.

You call it kin selection and file it. But kin selection is love of your own blood, the herd guarding its own genes. This is the man who is not your blood, who is marked, who is catching, who can never give you a single thing back and might take from you the one thing you have. Your animal was built, from the first cell, to run from him. You have his warmth in your hands right now, off your knees, godless, and you are calling it the animal, and you caught it a long way downstream off a hand you keep swearing to me is not there.

> I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven.
> Luke 10:18

**Voss.** I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven. And I can tell you what that is to the decimal, and it costs me nothing, watch my hands stay still.

It is a man in the first century with no germ theory, no tectonics, no idea what a virus is, reaching up into the sky to explain his bad luck with a spirit, the same tired reflex that put a god behind the thunder and a demon in the fever and a face on the moon. It is prescientific. It is childhood. We grew out of it, or the honest ones did.

There is no accuser up in the clouds getting knocked down. You will not catch me flinching at the word Satan, or demon, or angel, or any of the furniture. I have a drawer for the devil, and it is a wide one, and it is nearly full, and it has never in forty years cost me a single hour of sleep.

**Ames.** No devil in the clouds. Agreed. Put the pitchfork back in the drawer. But you skipped the word, and you never skip a word, so let me hold it up. Ho satanas. It is not a name first. It is a word, and the word is the accuser. The prosecutor. The one who stands up and points.

So watch what the thing actually is, underneath the horns you are so proud of not believing in. A crowd, frightened, coming apart, everyone against everyone, and then a voice moves through it and lands on one man and says there, him, that one, he is why. And the miracle happens, the real one, the one that runs every human society you have ever studied. The crowd goes quiet. The crowd goes calm. The crowd, for the first time in the crisis, is united, all of them turned one way, against the one, and the peace that floods the town the second they have their man is the sweetest peace they know, and it is built on a body. That voice, the one that finds the one and gathers the many against him, that is the accuser. That is the thing this man says he watched drop out of the sky like lightning, which is to say fast, and final, and lighting up the whole field for one second so you can see what was always standing there.

Now. You told me you walked out of the church into clean cold reason, and I believe you left the church. But there are two of those, Voss, two reasons wearing the one word, and you never once noticed you had only ever been handed the one. There is the reason that comes at the truth to be changed by it, that goes quiet and glad when it is corrected, that would honestly rather lose the argument and gain the thing. And there is the reason that comes at the truth to swing it, that finds the guilty sentence and lifts it over its head and brings it down on a man and feels the room turn its way as he goes down. One of those is rare and almost nobody carries it. The other one is the oldest altar on the earth, and it does not feel like an altar, it feels like winning.

Forty years. The debater with the sledgehammer, your phrase, you had it printed on you. The man who finds the one bad sentence and holds it up and lets the room lean in behind him as the other man falls. You did not walk out of religion into reason. You walked off one altar and onto the oldest one, and you have been the voice that moves through the crowd and finds the one to throw, and you called it the truth, and the lightning has been lighting you up this whole time, and you have had a drawer open, waiting for it, marked prescientific.

> Father, if you are willing, take this cup from me; yet not my will, but yours be done.
> Luke 22:42

**Voss.** The garden. Take this cup from me. Even HE did not want it. Read it. He is on his face in the dirt, sweating it, the text says like blood, begging his way out, asking three times, and I understand that man from the inside better than any believer who has ever lived.

Because there is no plan in that garden. There is no serene Son of God working the Father's clockwork. There is a body dumping adrenaline into the black and a terrified young man who can see, exactly, what the sunrise is going to do to him.

And I know that fear. I have stood on the very lip of it, in the dark, doing the math on my own death. We all have. It is the most honest second in the entire book. And it is the most ordinary second in the entire book. And by now, if you have listened to one word I have said, you can see those are not two facts. They are one fact. He is us. That is all he ever was. Us, scared, in a garden, in the dark.

**Ames.** Yes. He did not want it. Do not run past that, you ran past it just now to get to us is scared in a garden. Come back. It is the hinge everything hangs on.

Let me show you what every other version of me is, awake and asleep, and you already know this one, because you are the one who taught it to me. A hand, closing. Reaching for the thing the other man has and gripping it, the seat, the name, the win, the last word, because if I am holding what he has then maybe I am somebody, maybe I am real, maybe the speck stops being a speck. You said it in your own lecture, out of your own mouth, we grab at the world because we are tiny and it eases the terror of being tiny to have something in the fist. That is the whole of us. Reaching, gripping, afraid of the open hand. Every rivalry you ever had, every man you ever needed to beat, was you trying to get your being off of him because you could not find it in yourself.

Now look at this one. Here is the single life in the whole story that could have kept its grip. That could have reached up in that garden and closed its hand on the actual thing, the throne, the untouchable body, the legions of angels he says are standing right there, all of it, his, one word and it is his. And with all of it hanging in front of him in the dark, he opens the hand. Not my will. He lets it go. He reaches for nothing. He takes his being off of no one, robs no one, needs to beat no one, because he is the one man alive who is not trying to become somebody at somebody else's cost.

And that, only that, is why he is the one free man in a garden full of sleepers, all of them, even asleep, still clutching. You spent your life explaining the grip. You called it wiring, a bug we are stuck with, the animal in the fist. He is the one place the wiring came open, the one hand in the whole book that unclenched, and you walked right up to it, the only open hand you will ever be shown, and you looked at it and called it adrenaline.

> He saved others, but he can't save himself! Let him come down now from the cross, and we will believe in him.
> Matthew 27:42

**Voss.** Come down, they are screaming, come down off it and we will believe. He saved others, he cannot save himself. And he does not come down. And I have the cause, clean as a bell.

He does not come down because there is no one up there to reach in and lift him off the nails. That is the whole of it. And the silence, Ames, the silence that comes when the crowd finally, openly, in daylight, in front of everyone, asks for the one thing that would settle it, that silence is not a mystery. It is the answer. It is THE answer. It is the exact same flat nothing I have gotten back every single time in my life I asked that sky for one thing more than weather.

There is nothing to explain on that hill. Gravity, iron, the weight of a grown man on two nails, and a sky that does what the sky always does, which is nothing. It is the most complete thing I own, and it is the emptiest thing I own, and those, again, are one thing.

**Ames.** Then hear the actual words, because you flew over them to get to the silence. Come down and we will believe. Save yourself. Get down off that thing and prove to us that nothing can touch you, and then, once you have shown us you cannot be hurt, then we will bow.

Look at the god they are asking him to be. Untouchable. Unhurtable. Needing nothing, bleeding for nothing, up above it, safe. The strongman on the throne who proves he is a god precisely by the fact that nothing in the world can lay a hand on him. And Voss, that is the god you already threw out, and you were dead right to throw him out, that god is a bully with better lighting. The whole crowd is roaring at him to become the one god you and I both know is not worth believing in.

And he will not. That is the thing. He will not come down. He turns down the one move that ends the argument on the spot and wins the crowd in a breath, because to take it he would have to agree with the mob about what a god is. He would rather stay on the nails and be the one who can be hurt than come down and be their strongman who cannot.

Now hold that against your own life, because you have lived on the other side of this word your whole career. Nothing can touch me. I reasoned my way clear of hell, clear of death, clear of the void, one by one, calm, in daylight. I am immune. You are proud of the immunity the way Peter was proud of it, standing there hours before the rooster, swearing the others might fall but never me, never, I am the one they cannot break. And you know how that particular sentence ends, you have taught it, you have read it aloud. It ends with a man weeping in a courtyard at the sound of a bird.

The one who says nothing can touch me is always, always the one already leaning out over the edge of the fall. You said you had reasoned your way past all of it, and then a hillside in Colombia came down in the dark and buried thirty thousand people while they slept, and you were on the floor, and you have been on the floor about it for forty years. The immunity was paint too. The one man in the story who would not claim it is the one you are standing under, calling empty, in a coat you forgot you had on.

> My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?
> Matthew 27:46

**Voss.** And then the end of it, and here, here I put the drawer down. My God, my God, why have you forsaken me. God himself, if you have to use the word, nailed up, screaming it into the sky, and the sky gives him back nothing.

And I do not file this one, Ames, and I want you to notice, this is the one place in the whole book I stop working and just say yes. This one is true. This is the truest line any human being has ever set down in any book in any language, because it is the only sentence in all of Scripture that fits, seam for seam, no daylight between it and the world I actually wake up into every single morning. A good man, the best of them, screaming why into an empty room and getting the weather.

And it is not one man on one hill, do not shrink it. That is everybody's real prayer. That is the one underneath all the polite ones, the one you pray when the diagnosis comes, the one the mother prays over the small box. The true prayer. The only fully honest prayer a human being ever prays. And it went unanswered. He got nothing. We get nothing. That is not my argument against God, I am years past arguing. That is just the transcript of the room, read it back, there is no other voice on the tape.

**Ames.** I have nothing to fill that silence, and I am not going to hand you a warm thing to fill it, you would be right to throw it back and I would deserve the throw. Two things only, and then I will stop talking and stand in the dark next to you, because that is the honest place and I am not going to pretend I am somewhere else.

First. Listen to your own word. Forsaken. You called it forsaken. You called it wrong, a good man handed nothing, a thing that should not be. And I want to know where you got the ruler you just measured that with. Because the sky does not think a dying man is wrong, the sky has watched a billion of them and never flinched once. The animal does not think it is a scandal, the animal walks off and eats. The verdict that the innocent one must not be left alone in the dark, the whole weight burning in the word forsaken, you got it off this cross, off this exact wood, and you are swinging the ruler this scene gave you at the man who is bleeding on it.

Second. He was quoting. That scream is not a loose howl torn out of him, it is the first line of a song, the twenty-second Psalm, and every Jew on that hill who caught the first line felt the whole thing start to move under it. And it goes down. All the way down, further than you would think, the bones out of joint, the tongue stuck to the roof of the mouth, the men below him gambling for his clothes, which they were doing at that exact moment under his feet, so that he is narrating his own death out of a song a thousand years older than him. And then, at the very bottom, past the place where any honest man would let the song end, it turns. Not into rescue. He is not rescued. It turns into the strangest words in the whole of it, that the afflicted one cried and was not despised, was heard, and that a people not yet born, us, tonight, would be told what was done here.

A scream that sounds exactly like the empty sky. With an address on the back of the envelope.

It does not feed one starving child tonight. I keep saying that to you, over and over, because it is true and I will not lie to you to win a single inch of ground, I would rather lose you honest than keep you with a lie. It is a thin thread. That is all it is. He asked why too. He asked your question, your exact question, in words older than both of us. I am holding the thread in the dark and I am tired, and on the worst nights it is the only thing, the single only thing, that has ever kept me from setting the whole cup down for good.

> Put your finger here; see my hands. Reach out your hand and put it into my side. Stop doubting and believe.
> John 20:27

**Voss.** And now the resurrection, and I know you are holding it back to lay down last, the card that takes the whole hand. So lay it down. Because they cannot even agree on it. The four of them, the four foundation stones of the whole thing, and they cannot keep the story straight.

Matthew marches them up to Galilee, seventy miles north, a mountain, the risen man waiting. Luke bolts them to Jerusalem and will not let them out of the city, same day, stay here, do not leave. A hundred miles apart on the most important morning in the history of the species, and they cannot agree on the CITY. Cannot agree who reached the tomb first, who was inside it, how many, what was said.

And I have the cause and it is the same cause as the seed, the same cause as the girl, the same cause as all of it. Men, telling a story, and the story drifting the way every story drifts across a hundred retellings and forty years, a detail here, a whole city there. There is nothing in this a rumor cannot carry the entire distance on its own legs. Nothing.

I have accounted for the empty tomb exactly the way I accounted for the smallest of all seeds. And I want you to look at the account, really look at it, because it is seamless, it is total, there is not one thread left hanging out of it anywhere, and the completeness of it, the fact that I can close the single most important claim in human history and have NOTHING left over, that is the thing I am proudest of in this whole world.

**Ames.** Then let me stand exactly on the thing you are proudest of, because that is the precise spot where you went blind, and here is the cruelty of it, you are the only man alive with the training to see it.

You spent your whole life on forgeries. So answer your own question. What does a coordinated lie look like on the page. You know this better than any priest, any bishop, any believer breathing. It looks clean. It looks seamless. It agrees with itself down to the chairs in the room and the light through the window, because the entire point of sitting down to lie together is to get the story straight before anyone asks. The forger's whole gift is that there is no seam.

Four men who sat in a back room and agreed to sell a resurrection would have agreed on the city. They would have agreed on the morning. They would have picked one tomb and one angel and one order of events and drilled it cold. That is what men do when they are building a lie, and you know it, you built your career on the ones who did it and the tells they left in the mortar.

So four accounts that cannot agree on the city, that trip over each other about who ran to the tomb and who was inside and what the women were told, are not four men in a back room. They are the opposite of four men in a back room. They are the mess that only ever comes off of something nobody sat down and planned.

And now take the last step, in your own discipline, in your own voice, because this is the whole of what I have. Your clean version. Your seamless, total, airtight, nothing-left-over account of the most important morning in history, the one you are proudest of. That is not the shape of the truth, Voss. You, of all men, know what that shape is. That is the shape of the forgery. You built the one thing you spent forty years catching other men in, you built it with your own hands and set it around your own life, and you cannot find the seam in it for the same reason a man cannot see his own back. You are standing on the inside of the clean room, calling the clean room the world.

And look, one last time, at who the risen man walks toward. Not the believers. Not the ones who got it right, who kept the story straight, who deserved him. Thomas. The doubter. The one who folded his arms and said unless I put my own finger into the actual holes in the actual hands I will not believe one word, not from you, not from any of you. And the man does not throw him out of the room. Does not win the argument. Does not even really scold him. He walks the whole way across the floor to the one who cannot believe, and he holds his broken hands out into the open air, and he says here. Here. Put your finger here.

The doubter is not the villain at the end of the book. He is the one it crosses the whole room to reach. That is the chair you have been sitting in this entire time, arms folded, demanding the wounds. And I do not have a proof to put in your hand. I never had one, I told you that at the start and I meant it. I only have the thing the story will not stop doing, in every account, no matter which city they set it in and no matter how badly they keep the morning straight. It keeps walking toward the one who cannot believe. And it keeps holding out the wounds instead of the throne.

That is the two of them. I did not rig it.

Voss does not lose on the ground. He never loses on the ground. He has a drawer for every card, and half of what is in those drawers is just plainly true, and I will not insult either of us by pretending it is not.

The only thing Ames has on him, after all eight, is the shape of him. Everything explained. Nothing left over. Every thread tucked, every card filed, every drawer shut with that clean little click, and the door painted on the wall so well he stopped trying the handle years ago and started calling the room the world.

I stopped trusting that shape a long time ago. And here is the part I cannot get around. I first learned to distrust it in a believer who was certain of everything, who had an answer for my every doubt and a verse for my every wound. And I hear the exact same airlessness now in the man who is certain of nothing. It is the same sealed room with the paint switched.

I am certain of almost nothing. I carried one thread through eight verses and it did not snap. He asked why too. He opened his hand in the garden. He walked the whole floor to the doubter with the wounds held out.

That is not a proof, and I will not dress it as one. It is a direction. I am turned toward it in the dark and I am not letting go, and some nights, I will tell you as plain as I know how, that is the whole of the faith. All of it. One tired man who will not set the cup down.
