# The Crucifixion Satan Never Expected


> *None of the rulers of this age understood this, for if they had, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory.* (1 Corinthians 2:8)

If you read the history of mankind and look at the violence of the ancient days, you would see that every event rests on the same idea: the winner takes it all, and the winner is the one who writes history. [Hold onto that, because a myth is exactly that — history written by the winners, told over the body of the one they killed.]

For the cross, they thought to stifle the Word of Truth forever. But if you truly look at the story of the crucifixion, does it hold the same nature as a truth that has already been tampered with?

I want you to know that everyone knows the crucified man is cursed and cannot be the Messiah, for scripture says *cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree* (Deuteronomy 21:23). So for Christians to build their faith around the crucified one seems, at first, a bit idiotic. [If you wanted to invent a Messiah, this is the last death you would choose for him.]

It was not the fact that he was crucified that made them love him, but something else — which I am going to talk about.

[That something else is the resurrection.] Only when the disciples knew that this innocent victim was not simply like all the other thousands and millions who have been tortured and expelled and killed since the foundation of the world — only when they experienced him as the risen one, and confirmed that he is indeed who he said he is — was a new religious vision and a new set of values fully born in human history. What began like a mustard seed, like a bit of heaven, now begins to move through human history, from its Jewish beginnings out into the whole world.

The cross was a victory in the eyes of the Christians. Satan, duped by the Cross, will destroy his own kingdom and destroy himself. [But notice the problem:] the cross was not, in itself, a victory — we have already established that being crucified is bad on its own. Something *related* to it is what made it a victory.

## Now I want to talk about Satan

In John 8, Jesus says to those who oppose him: *Why is my language not clear to you? Because you are unable to hear what I say. You belong to your father, the devil, and you want to carry out your father's desires. He was a murderer from the beginning, not holding to the truth, for there is no truth in him. When he lies, he speaks his native language, for he is a liar and the father of lies.* (John 8:43–44)

[A murderer and a liar from the beginning — hold those two words together, because the whole mechanism is in them: the killing, and the lie that hides it.]

Satan makes his first named appearance in scripture in Job 1–2 and Zechariah 3:1–2, where *ha-satan* is a title before it is a name. It means the accuser, the prosecutor who stands up to bring the charge. [And this is worth pausing on:] the Holy Spirit is called the Paraclete — the one who is called to your side to help, to defend, to advocate. [Scripture sets the two against each other: the accuser on one side, the advocate on the other.]

Revelation 12:10 seals it: *the accuser of our brothers, who accuses them before our God day and night.*

Satan is accusation itself. By this simple understanding of Satan, you can see that the very act of accusing is reserved for him — it is who he is and what he does.

## The system they did not understand

[Now look at the cross with that understanding in hand.]

*None of the rulers of this age understood it, for if they had, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory.* (1 Corinthians 2:8)

*And having disarmed the powers and authorities, he made a public spectacle of them, triumphing over them by the cross.* (Colossians 2:15)

[And here is the line that holds the whole reversal:] the powers are not put on display because they are defeated — they are defeated because they are put on display.

*You spoke by the Holy Spirit through the mouth of your servant, our father David: "Why do the nations rage and the peoples plot in vain? The kings of the earth rise up and the rulers band together against the Lord and against his anointed one." Indeed Herod and Pontius Pilate met together with the Gentiles and the people of Israel in this city to conspire against your holy servant Jesus, whom you anointed. They did what your power and will had decided beforehand should happen.* (Acts 4:25–28)

*Then Satan entered Judas, called Iscariot, one of the Twelve.* (Luke 22:3)

You see? The gospels are saying that the people are operating under a system they do not understand, yet operate flawlessly — and the cross came to destroy that system and open the way to a new order, against the order of the old, against the wisdom of the elders.

## Myth and mechanism

[Here is the heart of it.] The crucifixion of Jesus is a victim mechanism like all the others — yet its outcome is different.

The thesis was never that the Gospels do not contain the persecution structure. It is the reverse: the Gospels are built out of the *same* persecution material as myth. The same lumber.

A myth says the victim was guilty and the crowd was right. That is it — that is the whole difference, when you compare a myth to the cross. *Jesus is innocent, and those who crucify him are guilty.* Nine words, and that is the entire axis of the inversion: in a myth, the community is justified in its lynching of the victim; here, it is not.

[And once you see that, you can name the voice doing the accusing.] Mimetic violence pronounces the guilt of the victim and the innocence of the persecutors. The community stops speaking, and the accuser speaks. Which means the myth's verdict — *victim guilty, crowd right* — is, quite literally, Satan talking.

[The structure is recognizable, like a fingerprint.] The persecution myth is a stereotype you can smell when you read it: a fixed template that recurs identically across texts that never met each other — the crisis, the difference-erasing crime, the marks on the victim, the all-against-one. Run Oedipus and the Passion through that same list and you tick every box in both. And the structure is specific, not just "violence" — a war chronicle is violent and carries none of this.

[There is even a spectrum.] Persecution texts are easier to decode than myth, because the victim's transfiguration is incomplete — the lynching still shows through the surface. The mechanism cannot fully erase its own traces; it leaves residue in the text itself. Myth hides the mechanism most, the persecution record half-exposes it, and the Gospel exposes it completely.

## The myth that never formed

So here is the test. [Imagine Satan had won.] If Satan had succeeded, he would have retold the crucifixion the way a myth is forced to tell it: Jesus a real blasphemer, his death rightly ending the unrest, Jerusalem at peace again, the man sacralized later.

We have none of it.

We have its mirror image. The myth that should have formed never formed — and that absence is the fingerprint of the mechanism breaking.
