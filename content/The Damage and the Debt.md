title: The Damage and the Debt
---

> *For God knows that when you eat from it your eyes will be opened, and you will be like God, knowing good and evil.* (Genesis 3:5)

Most arguments about original sin go wrong in the first move, because both sides agree to treat the Garden as a story about a rule. God posted a rule, they broke the rule, God punished the breach, and now everyone born afterward is on the hook for a violation they were not present for. Stated that way it is obviously unjust, and the atheist is right to say so, and the Christian ends up defending a legal fiction he does not really believe either.

The story is not about a rule. You can tell because of what the tree is called.

## The tree is named for the thing at stake

It is not the tree of disobedience. It is not a tree with nothing special about it, placed there arbitrarily so that obedience could be tested in the abstract, which is how it usually gets taught. It is the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, and the serpent's pitch names the prize exactly: you will be like God, knowing good and evil.

God already knows good and evil, but from the outside, as the one who defines it. That is what it means to be the reference point. The offer on the table is not information. It is the position. Know it yourself, from yourself, and stop needing the source.

So the choice in the Garden is a choice about where reality is measured from. God is saying, I am the center, I define what is good, trust me. The serpent is saying, you can be the center, define it yourself, be independent.

They took the second option. That is the first sin, and it is not disobedience to an arbitrary command. It is self-enthronement, moving yourself into the position where God was and sourcing your meaning, your morality and your identity from inside yourself.

Once you see it that way the rest of the doctrine stops being a legal puzzle and becomes something more like a diagnosis.

## What actually died

God says that on the day they eat of it they will certainly die. They eat, and they do not fall over. So either the warning failed or something died that we are not looking at.

Read the next scene closely, because the whole answer is in the staging.

Their eyes are opened and they know they are naked. Before this they were naked and unashamed, which is a description of a state, not a note about clothing. It is total transparency, with God, with each other, with themselves. Nothing hidden, nothing performed, no self-consciousness, because the self did not have to be constructed. It was received. They were a lamp lit by a current from outside them.

Then, in one verse, three things happen in sequence. They become self-conscious. They make coverings for themselves, which is the first act of building an identity from the inside out. And they hide from God.

That is the death, and it happened on the day exactly as promised. Not the annihilation of the spirit, which is why nothing visible occurred. The severing of a connection. The lamp is not destroyed when you unplug it. It just goes dark, and it goes dark instantly.

The hiding is the proof, and it is the detail I keep coming back to. Nobody hides from someone they are still in relationship with. Hiding is what you do when the relationship is already over and you have not said so yet.

Physical death is the second thing, and it follows from the first rather than being added on top of it. They were sustained by a communion they no longer had, and they are put outside the reach of the tree of life, and what was never designed to sustain itself begins the long return to dust. Death arrives as the consequence of disconnection, not as a sentence handed down for a technical infraction.

I should flag one thing here, because the popular version of this argument overreaches. People make a great deal of the Hebrew of Genesis 2:17, *moth tamuth*, literally dying you shall die, and read the doubling as a description of a process that begins immediately and culminates later. That reading is available, but the construction is an ordinary Hebrew intensifier, and its plain force is emphatic, you shall surely die. The theological point stands on the narrative, which shows you exactly what died and when. It does not need the grammar, and it is weaker when you make it lean on grammar that will not hold the weight.

## The shape of the damage

The Latin name for what is left is *incurvatus in se*, curved in on itself.

That is the piece worth having, because it says that sin is not first a list of bad actions. It is a posture of the whole being. Human nature was oriented outward, receiving what it is from beyond itself. Now it is bent inward, trying to be its own source of worth and meaning and identity, and every particular sin is an expression of that bend rather than an isolated event.

And here is where this connects to everything else on this site, because a nature curved inward is exactly the nature that cannot keep the tenth commandment. If you are your own reference point, you have no way to know what is worth wanting, so you find out by watching your neighbor. His wanting becomes your instruction. That is the whole mechanism I work through in [[The Commandment You Cannot Obey]], and it is why the tenth commandment is the one that convicts you while you are sitting still. The curve is not a separate doctrine from mimetic desire. The curve is why desire has to be borrowed.

## Guilt or consequence

Christians divide here, and the division is real, though it is regularly described as being sharper than it is.

The Western tradition, following Augustine, treats Adam as the legal representative of the race. His act implicates those who descend from him, and what we inherit includes original guilt, a state of estrangement carrying the sentence his act incurred, along with disordered desire, mortality, and the loss of a grace that was never natural equipment in the first place. The picture is a revoked access, a pass into a room that the family no longer holds.

The Eastern tradition says we inherit the consequences and not the guilt. A wounded nature, not a criminal record. Mortality as the environment we are born into rather than a punishment assigned to us. Separation from God's life, not because we are being sentenced for someone else's act, but because the communion was broken before we arrived and we are born outside it. The analogy that gets used is a child born to an addicted mother, who did not choose the damage, is not guilty of it, and carries it anyway.

I find the Eastern framing more honest to the text and less trouble to defend, and I will say plainly that inherited guilt for an act you did not commit is the version I cannot make work morally. But I want to resist the tidy contrast, because both traditions affirm a damaged nature, both deny that we are punished for Adam's personal act as though it were ours, and much of the apparent distance is Augustine's Latin doing work his Greek contemporaries did in other words. The dispute is narrower than the tribal version of it.

What both hold, and what matters for the next part, is that each person then confirms the inheritance personally. You do not merely have Adam's problem. You add your own. We are both the inheritors of a break we did not make and the authors of our own.

## Two problems, not one

That is the structure the atonement has to answer, and once it is laid out the pieces stop looking arbitrary.

There is a damaged nature, inherited, bent inward, not your fault in the way your own acts are your fault, and real. And there is a personal ledger, your own choices, your own debt.

The sacrificial system addressed the second and never claimed to address the first. Specific sins, specific offerings, specific blood, and the whole thing had to be done again next year. The repetition is the tell. A system that repeats annually is a system that covers rather than cures. It settles accounts and leaves the accountant unchanged.

The cross is aimed at both layers, and it does something different to each.

To the ledger it does what a substitution does. The debt is paid, the sentence served by someone else, and the transaction does not need repeating.

To the nature it does something stranger, and this is where the Eastern emphasis earns its place. God became human. A human nature that never once curved inward, that walked through the entire human situation, temptation and exhaustion and abandonment and death, without collapsing into self-reference. The bend was reversed from inside, by a human, in human material. And then that human died, and death had no legitimate claim on him, and when he came out the other side he brought the nature through with him.

That is why the resurrection is not a happy ending appended to a transaction. In the ledger frame it is superfluous, because the debt was settled at the cross and the rest is confirmation. In the nature frame it is the actual repair. It is the demonstration that the consequence of the original choice runs backward, that the disconnection can be reversed, that dust is not the last word about what we are made of.

## Why you still sin

Which explains the thing that bothers converts most, and bothered me.

If the nature is healed, why is nothing different on Tuesday?

Because a nature is not repaired the way a debt is cleared. The debt is a transaction and transactions complete. The nature is more like a bone that has been set. The setting is finished, the healing is real and has actually begun, and the bone is still tender and will be for a long time. The direction changed. The destination is secured. You are walking through the recovery and not standing at the end of it.

This is also, I think, the only sane account of ascetic practice. Fasting and prayer and the rest are not payment and not merit. They are what you do after surgery, which is to remove the things that slow the healing and put yourself where the healing happens. Nobody thinks physical therapy performed the operation. Nobody thinks it is optional either.

And the whole of it turns on the first move, the one in the Garden that looked like a rule and was not. Someone has to be the reference point. The offer was that it could be you. The damage is what happens to a creature that accepts, and the repair is a Person coming to stand in the position again, in the flesh, where you can see him, which is the argument I make at length in [[The Tenth Commandment and the Incarnation]].
