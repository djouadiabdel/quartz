---
title: The Spectrum of Legibility
---

*Case File 05. The spectrum.*

Every founding text hides a body. The only question is how well.

That sounds like an accusation and it is not meant as one. It is closer to a structural observation about how communities come to exist. Go back far enough in the story a people tells about its own origin and you tend to find a death at the root of it, and you tend to find the death handled rather than reported. Rome has the murdered brother. Cities across the ancient world have a founder buried in the wall or under the gate. The killing is not incidental to the founding. It is the founding.

## Traces

A killing at the center of a story leaves traces, and traces can be more or less legible.

They are hard to erase completely because the account has to explain too many things at once. It has to explain why the community exists, why the victim mattered, why there is a shrine, why the date is observed, why the peace came when it did. Each of those requires keeping some part of what happened, and the parts do not always cohere.

So instead of asking whether a text hides a lynching, which flattens everything into yes or no, arrange texts on an axis. How much of the machine can you still see through the surface?

## The scale

**Myth. Hides it fully.** The victim becomes a monster or becomes a god, and either move accomplishes the same thing. If he was a monster, killing him was hygiene. If he became a god, the killing was a mystery and a gift. The lynching disappears in both directions. There is no crowd in the account, or the crowd is an instrument of the gods, and the death reads as fate rather than as something people did with their hands. This is the fully processed end of the scale.

**Persecution record. Half hidden.** A witch trial transcript, an account of a pogrom written by the town that carried it out. These still read as justice from the inside. The clerk believed the woman was a witch. But the machinery shows through, because the document is administrative and administrative documents record too much. You can see the plague or the failed harvest in the background. You can see the accusation naming exactly the crisis the town was suffering. You can see the neighbors, the confession under torture, the unanimity. Nobody in the room was reading it as we do. We can read it because the paperwork kept more than it meant to.

The historical distance is doing the work there. We can decode a witch trial because we no longer share its beliefs about witches. We are outside the crowd, and being outside is what makes the pattern visible.

**One case. Fully exposed.** The victim is simply innocent, and the text says so, out in the open, at the time, in the account its own community kept.

## The last position on the scale

The third position is odd in a way worth stating precisely.

The first two positions are explained by distance. A myth is opaque to the people inside it and legible to us. A persecution record is half legible because the clerk's beliefs are dead and ours are not yet dead. In both cases the decoding is done by the reader, from outside, later.

In the third case the decoding is inside the document. The text is not opaque and waiting for a modern reader to see through it. It names the victim innocent and the crowd wrong, in its own voice, without needing anyone's hindsight. The reading that took the rest of us two thousand years and a change of civilization to reach is already written down in the account itself, by people who were standing inside the crowd.

## What that costs it

And here is the part that makes this file worth writing.

Every other founding story needs its victim to stay guilty in order to keep working. That is not a moral failing of those stories. It is a functional requirement. The guilt is what converts a murder into an act of order, and the act of order is what founds the community. Take the guilt away and the founding does not hold.

This one dismantles the very mechanism that founds it. It publishes the machine. It tells its readers, in detail, exactly how a crowd selects a victim, exactly what unanimity feels like from inside, exactly what the peace afterward is worth. And it does this in the document the community is built on.

## The question

Every institution protects the thing that makes it possible. That is nearly a definition of an institution.

So why would a text expose the machine that made it?
