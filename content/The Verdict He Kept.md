---
title: The Verdict He Kept
---

Let me say up front that Bart Ehrman is not the TikTok guy. He is not recycling atheist Reddit talking points. He spent decades in Greek and Hebrew, he was born again at fifteen, he was a pastor, he prayed, he spoke in tongues, he memorized whole books of the New Testament word for word. When he tells you he was sincere, believe him. So we owe this one an actual answer and not a dunk.

But here is the thing that made me sit up. Ehrman tells us exactly where his deconversion started, and it started with the smallest of all seeds. Literally. He points to Mark chapter 4, the mustard seed, the same verse the TikTok crowd throws around, and he says that was the first little crack. Small contradictions, he calls them, seeds that grow into a big bush. And he is honest enough to name the emblem himself.

Now watch what happens in the very same chapter he is quoting from. A few verses after the mustard seed, Matthew tells us why Jesus spoke in parables at all.

> So was fulfilled what was spoken through the prophet: "I will open my mouth in parables, I will utter things hidden since the creation of the world."
> Matthew 13:35

Older translations render that last line "things hidden since the foundation of the world." René Girard took those exact words and built his most important book on them. Ehrman stares at the seed in Mark 4 and walks straight past the sentence in Matthew 13 that tells you what the whole harvest is for. That is the entire argument of this essay in miniature. He kept looking at the seed and missed what it came to uncover.

## Give him his best point, because it is a good one

Half of Ehrman's case is aimed at a target Christianity never had to defend. He lines up the contradictions, the call of the disciples in Mark versus John, the temple cleansing early in John and late in the others, Jairus's daughter sick in Mark and already dead in Matthew, the date of the crucifixion. And his conclusion from all of it is that the Bible cannot be an inerrant, word for word, mistake free phone book dropped out of heaven.

Fine. He is right. And he is right in a way most of his critics are too nervous to admit. Inerrancy as he was taught it at Moody is a modern invention, late nineteenth century, a defensive crouch against Darwin and German scholarship. Ehrman even makes the point himself, and it is devastating to his old side, not to ours. The ancient creeds, the Apostles' Creed, the Nicene Creed, the things the whole church actually confessed, say nothing about believing in the Bible. Not one word. You believe in the Father, in Christ, in the Spirit. The book is the witness to the faith, not the object of it. Chesterton would have told him that for free.

So notice what has happened. Ehrman spent his first move proving that a mustard seed is not the whole tree. Which is precisely the mistake the TikTok critic makes with the seed itself. And by his own account this is not even why he left. He says it plainly. The scholarship moved him from fundamentalism to mainline Christianity and he stayed a believer for years. The contradictions were never the exit.

## The real reason, and it deserves better than a slogan

He left over suffering. Ten thousand children dead of starvation since yesterday. Ten thousand more by tomorrow. The 1918 flu, the Black Death, the mudslide in Colombia that killed thirty thousand people overnight while he was walking out the door to teach. He looked at that and he could no longer believe in a God who is at once all powerful, all loving, and awake.

I am not going to hand him a tidy theodicy, because the tidy ones are an insult and he is right to be furious at them. Tectonic plates so we can have life. The Holocaust to make the survivors more noble. Children starving so the rest of us are moved to act. He debated men who said these things to his face and he got angry, and good for him, because those answers are obscene.

But here is the question Girard forces, and it is the one Ehrman never turns on himself. Where did the outrage come from?

Because nature does not grieve. A universe of scattered molecules running down under the second law of thermodynamics has no opinion about a starving child. The ancient world Ehrman loves, the Greeks and Romans he now reads every morning for his devotions, left unwanted infants on hillsides to die of exposure and felt no scandal about it at all. The strong ruled, the weak went to the wall, and nobody wrote a book of Job about it. The conviction that the anonymous suffering of a far off child is an intolerable stain on the conscience of the cosmos is not a finding of biology. It is not written in the DNA. It arrived in history from somewhere.

Girard spent his life tracing where. The growing sensitivity to victims, the reflex that makes us take the side of the crushed and the excluded, is the slow leaven of the Gospel working through the West, long after people stopped believing the Gospel. Nietzsche saw it perfectly clearly and hated Christianity precisely for it, for taking the side of the victim against the strong. Dionysus versus the Crucified, he called it, and he knew which side the modern conscience had chosen.

Ehrman has picked up the church's own sword and is swinging it at the church. His rage on behalf of the innocent victim is not an argument against Christianity. It is Christianity, still burning in him after he let go of everything else.

## Watch him keep the ethic and cut the root

He tells you this himself, and I do not think he notices. In the humanist lecture he grounds morality in evolution. We love our children because our genes survive if we do. We help our kin because it propagates our DNA. Loving people, he says, is like liking chocolate ice cream, it is just how he is built, no logic required.

But then, two breaths later, he tells you what the actual moral law is. Care for the stranger. Care for the far off. Care for the poor, especially the poor. He even says, in his own words, that it was Jesus who redefined community to mean everybody, not just your family, not just your class, not just your city, but the outsider and the outcast. The Greeks and Romans did not teach that. Jesus did.

On strict Darwinian terms the starving stranger's child is nothing to your genes. Ehrman said so himself, that people who care about someone they will never know let their genes die out. So the one ethic he cannot get from his evolution, the love of the stranger who can do nothing for you, is the exact ethic he keeps and calls the heart of the good life. He kept the fruit and sawed off the branch and then used the branch to beat the trunk.

> The King will reply, "Truly I tell you, whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers and sisters of mine, you did for me."
> Matthew 25:40

That is where his humanism actually comes from. He is living on borrowed Gospel and calling it reason.

## The Gospels are not myth. They are the end of myth.

In the Q&A someone asks about the mustard, the mythicists, the pagan parallels. Ehrman says the strongest argument he would use, if he were a mythicist, which he is not, is that the Greek and Roman world is full of stories like the Jesus story. Miraculous births, miracle workers, men who rise and ascend. He grants that the Gospels look like myth.

Girard grants it too, and then turns it inside out. Yes, the Gospel takes up the same raw material as the myths, a crowd, a victim, a killing. But myth is told by the mob, and myth always hides one thing, the innocence of the victim. The myth says the victim was guilty, dangerous, rightly destroyed, and peace returned when he was gone. The Gospel tells the identical event from the other side, from the ground, from under the boot, and it says the victim was innocent and the crowd was wrong. That inversion is not a small literary difference. It is the demolition of the entire machine.

Listen to Caiaphas set the machine running in plain sight.

> It is better for you that one man die for the people than that the whole nation perish.
> John 11:50

There it is, the scapegoat formula spoken out loud, one victim to buy the crowd its peace, and the Gospel quotes it only to condemn it. Listen to Jesus name the thing exactly.

> They hated me without reason.
> John 15:25

Without reason. The victim is innocent and the text says so on the record. This is the thing hidden since the foundation of the world, the murder the myths were built to cover, dragged out into daylight. Cain and Abel is the first draft, the founding brother killing the founding brother and asking, "Am I my brother's keeper," and God answering that the blood is crying from the ground. The blood always cried. The Gospel is the first place the crowd is finally forced to hear it.

That is why the accuser falls.

> I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven.
> Luke 10:18

Satan, ho satanas, is the accuser, the voice of the mob, the mechanism that unites everyone against one and calls it justice. The Cross does not defeat him with a bigger army. It defeats him by exposure, by turning on the lights in the room where the machine only ever worked in the dark. Ehrman spent his whole career as a prosecutor, running the case against the text, and he never once noticed he was standing in a courtroom the Gospel built, using a verdict the Gospel handed him, defending a victim only the Gospel taught him to see.

## Chesterton already answered the suffering, and he answered it Ehrman's way

Here is the part Ehrman would hate and should love. He rejected N. T. Wright's answer, the one where all the suffering is folded into the grand plan and you are not allowed to complain about the trees because you have to look at the forest. He threw it out. And Chesterton would have thrown it out with him.

Orthodox Christianity never said the mudslide was the plan. Chesterton's picture of the world is occupied territory, a good thing gone wrong, a right thing broken, something you are meant to be loyal to and at war with at the same time. And that is exactly the position that lets you fight. The man who calls the starving child an intruder, an outrage, a wrongness that should not be, can wage war on it until he dies. The man who calls it just tectonic plates, just DNA, just entropy running its course has already handed in the word "evil" at the door.

And Ehrman will not hand it in. He ends his own lectures with a list. Starvation is an evil. Oppression is an evil. Homelessness, ignorance, war, all evil, and we should fight them. Where does a bag of molecules get the word evil? He kept it. Same as he kept the innocent victim, same as he kept the love of the stranger. He kept the whole moral verdict of Christianity and crossed out the name of the judge, and then he told us the courtroom was empty the whole time.

Chesterton had his number a century early. The madman, he wrote, is not the man who has lost his reason. The madman is the man who has lost everything except his reason. Ehrman is the debater still, airtight, consistent, the case perfectly argued, and somewhere out past the edge of the argument is the elephant of the thing he cannot stop believing, the innocent must not suffer, standing in broad daylight ten yards away.

He looked at the smallest of all seeds and watched it crack his fundamentalism. He should have kept reading to the end of the chapter. The seed he could not account for grew into the plant he is sheltering under. He kept the verdict. He just does not want to say Whose it is.
