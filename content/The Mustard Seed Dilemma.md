---
title: The Mustard Seed Dilemma
---

This goofy argument has been making the rounds on TikTok lately, the so-called mustard seed dilemma. Jesus called the mustard seed the smallest of all seeds, but actually poppy seeds and orchid seeds are smaller. So the argument goes like this. If Jesus is God, he can't make a scientific mistake. Jesus made a scientific mistake. Therefore Jesus isn't God. Or the gospel writers recorded his words wrong. Either way, Christianity supposedly has a big problem.

But this goofy argument only works if Jesus actually intended to give a technical ranking of every single seed species upon planet Earth. That is precisely what the critic needs to prove and not simply assume. Jesus was telling an agricultural parable about a man sowing a seed in his field. The setting is farming, planting, and garden growth, not global seed taxonomy.

## Read the actual sentence

Now yeah, we should admit that Mark's wording is strong. He says the mustard seed is smaller than all the seeds upon the earth.

> "It is like a mustard seed, which is the smallest of all seeds on earth. Yet when planted, it grows and becomes the largest of all garden plants, with such big branches that the birds can perch in its shade."
> Mark 4:31-32

But look at what he does in the very same breath. He describes it as being sown upon the ground and compares it with the other seeds upon the ground. The Greek word *ge* can mean earth, land, ground, or soil, while *pas*, meaning all, takes its scope from the context. So the word all doesn't automatically mean every single seed species existing anywhere on the planet.

Words like all, smallest, greatest operate within a universe of discourse supplied by the actual setting. And here that setting is a farmer sowing seeds into the ground and watching them grow into garden plants. The critic is assuming a global botanical comparison when the passage itself gives an agricultural one.

## The farming world Jesus was describing

The plant in view is black mustard, which was native to the Middle East. Scholar Gleason Archer notes that no one has demonstrated that in ancient Palestine farmers cultivated a plant with a smaller seed. So within the farming world Jesus was describing, mustard could naturally function as the smallest of the familiar seeds being planted. The critic is expanding Jesus' comparison way beyond the setting of the actual parable.

## Even if you take all broadly, it still fails

But even if all seeds is taken more widely, the objection still collapses. The mustard seed was proverbial in ancient Jewish speech for something extremely small. Jesus uses it the same way elsewhere when he speaks of faith like a mustard seed.

> "Because you have so little faith. Truly I tell you, if you have faith as small as a mustard seed, you can say to this mountain, 'Move from here to there,' and it will move. Nothing will be impossible for you."
> Matthew 17:20

He's invoking a familiar image of extreme smallness, not publishing a scientific classification.

And this proverbial language is not based on ignorance. Mishnah Niddah 5:2 speaks of an amount the size of a mustard seed or even smaller than that. Read that again. Jewish speakers could use the mustard seed as a benchmark for extreme smallness while openly recognizing in the same sentence that smaller amounts exist. That destroys the entire claim that this way of speaking requires ignorance. An omniscient Jesus is still allowed to speak in normal human language.

Imagine a football coach pointing to one player and saying, "He's the smallest guy on the field, but he plays like a giant." Nobody searches high and low across every single football field on planet Earth to find someone shorter. The setting tells you what's being compared, and the contrast is the entire point.

## They isolated one half of the sentence

Here is the part the critics keep walking past. They are isolating only one half of Jesus' comparison. The mustard seed was not chosen merely because it was tiny, but because something proverbially tiny could grow into the largest plant in the garden. The whole illustration is about a contrast between an insignificant beginning and an astonishing result. Small beginning, surprisingly great growth. That is the actual point of the parable.

Jesus said that his kingdom would begin like something tiny and easily ignored, but grow into something enormous. He began with a small group of fishermen and followers in an obscure quarter of the Roman Empire, and now his movement has stretched across the entire world.

To paraphrase C.S. Lewis, critics claim to see the mustard seed while missing the elephant of prophetic fulfillment ten yards away in broad daylight. They're staring at one word and missing the entire comparison.

There's no bad science from Jesus here. This is just effective communication. These days the crew pushing this stuff is so desperate that they're just recycling old atheist Reddit talking points.
