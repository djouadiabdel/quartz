---
title: Mimesis & the Cross
---

Essays reading the Gospels the way René Girard read them: as forensics, not sermons. The claim is simple and testable. Human violence runs on a hidden mechanism, all-against-one, and the Bible is the one ancient text that turns the lights on in the room where the mechanism works.

Start here:

- [[The Commandment You Cannot Obey]], the tenth commandment discovers mimetic desire, hits the wall no law can climb, and needs a Person to finish the sentence.
- [[The Crucifixion Satan Never Expected]], the cross as a victim mechanism like all the others, with one detail that breaks it.
- [[The Cross-Examination]], two men in my head who have never agreed on anything, given a night to argue it out with nobody refereeing.

On marriage and the household:

- [[Polygone|The Frame and the Rival]], a Chesterton and Girard reading of why polygamy builds a rivalry engine and monogamy is the frame that shuts it out.
- [[The Detonator]], why the child, not the wife, is where the household actually ignites, and what a house that survives it looks like.
- [[The Same Cup]], a confession, taking the marriage verses one at a time and pouring myself onto them until the contradiction shows.

The forensic cut:

- [[Anatomy of a Myth]], five case files that never argue for Christianity. Each one establishes a test on neutral ground, runs it on one more text, and stops on a question.

On the doctrine:

- [[The Damage and the Debt]], the Garden read as a choice about where reality gets measured from, and why the atonement has to answer two different problems.
- [[The Tenth Commandment and the Incarnation]], why a commandment aimed at the eyes needs a Person the eyes can follow.
- [[The Night We Argued the Trinity]], most of a night against a friend's best objections, including the ones I could not put down.
- [[Filioque|Filioque, One Word and Two Verbs]], two churches split over a translation, and the fight turns out to sit in a gap between two Greek verbs and nowhere else.

On method:

- [[Something That Can Say No]], eleven posts on a project that turned four of Girard's sentences into code, committed every prediction before the scorer existed, and published the twenty-two failures. The subject is not Girard. It is what it takes to build something that can tell you no.

On the evidence:

- [[The Checkable Parts]], what archaeology can and cannot buy you, with the overclaims cut out.
- [[Putting the Stone Back]], the trick underneath a careful skeptic's case for a tie.
- [[The Verdict He Kept]], Bart Ehrman named the verse his deconversion started with, and it is worth taking seriously.
- [[The Mustard Seed Dilemma]], the smallest objection on the internet, answered by reading the sentence it comes from.
- [[The Defense Is the Evidence]], what it means when a reading has to be bent to protect the doctrine that needs it.
