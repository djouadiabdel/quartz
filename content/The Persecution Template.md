*Case File 02. The template.*

Persecution stories are not random. That is the finding this case file rests on, and it is the sort of finding that sounds obvious once stated and is not obvious at all before.

When a community turns on somebody, the account it produces afterward tends to have the same parts, in the same order, whatever the century and whatever the culture. Different gods, different crimes, different names, same skeleton. It is fixed enough that you can write it down as a checklist and tick boxes.

## The checklist

Four items. Each one is a thing the text either has or does not have, and you can check it without knowing anything about the religion involved.

**One. A crisis grips the whole community.** Not one person's problem. Plague, famine, drought, civil collapse, something that has everyone by the throat at once and that nobody can trace to a cause.

**Two. A crime that erases distinctions.** The victim is accused of something specific, and the specific things recur. Incest, parricide, poisoning the wells, ritual murder of children, plotting against the state, blasphemy. Notice what those have in common. Every one of them destroys a difference that a society is built on. The difference between mother and wife, between father and stranger, between what is sacred and what is not. The accusation always names the collapse the community is actually living through.

**Three. A victim marked out as different.** Some sign that separates him from the crowd. A limp, a foreign accent, a disfigurement, being a foreigner, being a king, being too poor or too rich. The mark does not have to be a defect. It only has to be a mark. Anything that makes one person selectable out of a crowd will serve.

**Four. All against one, then peace returns.** The crowd acts as a unit, without a trial worth the name, and what follows is the strange part. The crisis lifts. The community is calm. Very often the victim is afterward treated as sacred, credited with the peace his death produced.

## Test one, Oedipus

Run the list.

Thebes is under plague, and nobody knows why. Box one.

The crimes charged are parricide and incest, the two that collapse every distinction inside a family. Box two.

The victim limps. He is named for the injury, and he is a foreigner in the city he rules. Box three.

The city turns on him as one, drives him out, and the plague ends. Afterward he becomes a figure of strange sanctity, a blessing on the ground that holds his grave. Box four.

Four ticks. And notice that the text never presents this as a lynching. It presents it as a discovery. Thebes had a pollution in it, the pollution was found, the pollution was expelled, the city was healed. The account is written from inside the crowd's own conviction that it was right.

## Test two

Same list, different text.

A crisis. An occupied province, a festival crowd packed into a city, an authority worried enough about riot to act fast.

A crime that erases distinctions. Blasphemy, which is exactly the collapse of the line between the human and the divine, the sharpest distinction that culture had.

A victim marked out. Provincial accent, no standing, a claim to kingship that makes him selectable in the one way that most reliably gets a man killed under occupation.

All against one. The crowd is explicitly made one, and the text says so. Two authorities who were enemies become friends the same day, over him. Then the crowd disperses and the festival ends.

Four boxes tick again.

## Except one refuses

Run the second box once more and read it closely.

In Oedipus, the crime is real. That is not incidental to the story, it is the story. He did kill his father and he did marry his mother. The text takes the trouble to establish the guilt, because the guilt is what makes the expulsion an act of justice rather than a murder, and the whole machinery of the play is built to deliver that finding.

In the second text the accusation is made, the machinery runs, the man is executed. And the account states, repeatedly and from every available angle, that he did not do it. The judge says he finds no basis for a charge. The soldier at the execution says it. One of the men dying beside him says it. The text does not present this as a discovery that the community made afterward and regretted. It presents the innocence as known at the time, by the people doing the killing.

Every other box holds. That one inverts.

## The question

Same machine, running the same sequence, with a single value flipped from guilty to innocent.

What flips it?
