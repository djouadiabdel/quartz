---
title: Filioque, One Word and Two Verbs
---

I thought this was a fight about the Holy Spirit. It is a fight about a word. Two churches split over a translation, and once you see the translation you cannot unsee it.

Here is the whole thing before the history. Greek has two verbs where Latin has one. The split lives in that gap and nowhere else.

## The two verbs

The strong verb is *ekporeuesthai*. It is the word in John 15:26, the Spirit "who proceeds from the Father." In Greek it does not mean the loose comes-from. It means to have your source, your cause, your very existence out of someone. It names the fountainhead.

The soft verb is *proienai*. To go forth, to shine out, to be sent. It does not name a cause. It names manifestation, the shining, the radiance.

The Father is the cause of the Spirit. That is the strong verb, and it takes one subject only. The Father.

The Spirit shines and rests and is sent through the Son. That is the soft verb, and the Greeks were always happy to run it through the Son. "From the Father, through the Son." Gregory says it. Damascene says it. Nobody fought about it.

Then Latin came with one word for both jobs. *Procedit*. And a Greek reading the creed heard the strong verb where the Latin meant the soft one. That is the whole war. A mistranslation colliding with itself for a thousand years.

## The verses each side holds

The West did not invent this out of nothing. They had verses and they read them one way.

The Spirit is called the Spirit of the Son. The Spirit of Christ. Galatians 4:6. Romans 8:9. Christ breathes on the disciples and says receive the Holy Spirit. John 20:22. The Son sends him. John 15:26 again, "whom I will send to you."

If the Spirit is the Son's own, eternally, then the Son must be in his procession. That is the Latin reading. The possessive reaches back into the being of God.

The East had the same verses and put them in a different drawer. Every one of them is the mission. The sending in time. Or at most the shining, the manifestation. "Spirit of the Son" means the Spirit rests on the Son and shines from him. It does not mean the Son caused him. The verses describe the radiance, not the origin.

So both sides quote scripture and both are honest. They disagree about what the verses are describing. Origin, or manifestation. That is the seam.

## The history, fast

It starts with a heretic named Eunomius. He said the essence of God is unbegottenness. One word that names what God is. And since the Son is begotten, the Son does not share the essence. The Son is unlike the Father. That was Arianism with a dictionary, and the stakes were the divinity of Christ.

The Cappadocians killed it. Basil, and the two Gregories. They said you cannot build a nature out of a negation. Unbegotten is a from-no-one. It tells you how the Father exists, not what he is. It is a mode, not an essence. Hold that. It comes back at the end and it is the key to everything.

Then Augustine, in the West, writing *On the Trinity*. He builds the engine that will drive the Filioque. I will get to the engine. It is the real thing under the whole dispute.

The word Filioque, "and the Son," first gets bolted onto the creed in Spain. Local councils at Toledo, fighting their own Arians, wanting to say the Son is fully God by making the Spirit come from him too. It spreads through the Franks. Charlemagne pushes it. Rome resists it for a while and then gives in.

The East sees a church adding words to a creed that a council forbade anyone to change. In the ninth century Photios, patriarch of Constantinople, writes the hard case against it. In 1054 the legates and the patriarch excommunicate each other. The word is not the only cause but it is the one they name.

And in the middle of all this, one man saw the answer. Maximus the Confessor, seventh century, told the Greeks not to panic. He said the Romans do not mean the strong verb. By *procedit* they do not make the Son a cause. They only confess the Spirit goes through the Son. Maximus is the peace, and everyone stopped listening to him for four hundred years.

## Augustine's engine

This is the part I kept getting wrong, so I will go slow.

In God everything is common. One nature, one will, one power. The only thing that is not shared is the relation of origin, the from and the to. So the only thing that can tell one person from another is a relation. A source and a sourced.

Now look at the Son and the Spirit. Both come from the Father. If that is all you say, what tells them apart? Nothing. They are both just the-one-from-the-Father, and they collapse into each other.

So Augustine concludes there has to be a relation between the Son and the Spirit themselves. The Spirit must come from the Son too, or you cannot tell the two apart. The Filioque is not a second doctrine. It is the output of that one argument.

That is the West at its deepest. Not a verse. A logic. No two persons are distinct except by an opposition, a rope running from one to the other. Son and Spirit need a rope, so the Spirit is from the Son.

## The Greek answer

The East says you do not need the rope.

The Son and the Spirit are told apart by their mode of coming. One is begotten. One proceeds. Two different ways of being from the one Father. That is already two. You do not need a line running between them. You need only that they come differently.

Then Augustine presses. Fine. What is the difference between begotten and proceeding? Name it.

And here the Greeks stop. They cannot. Damascene flat out. We know that they differ. We do not know how. The mode of generation and the mode of procession are past us. There is a real distinction and it is a mystery.

So both sides are in the dark about the same thing. Neither can say what begetting is, or what proceeding is, or what unbegottenness is. All three are unspeakable. I thought Augustine explained more than the Greeks. He does not. He explains nothing about the what. Nobody does.

## Where it actually splits

Strip away everything and one question is left. It is not a question about verses. It is about an axiom.

Is a bare difference of mode enough to make two persons two. Or does it take a relation of opposition.

The Greek axiom is smaller. It asks for less. It says begotten and proceeding are already two, do not ask me how, the Father alone is the source and that is worth more than your tidy logic.

Augustine's axiom is bigger. It says a difference you cannot ground is a difference you are only asserting. To make it real you need an opposition, and the only one left is Son to Spirit. His reward is he can say what makes the persons two. His cost is a rope that looks to the East like a second cause.

Logic does not pick between them. That is why nobody has ever been argued out of it. It is not a mistake. It is a choice of first principle. The West bought explanation with an extra axiom. The East bought a smaller axiom with a mystery.

## The tree

Gregory of Nyssa has an image and it settles the whole feeling of it.

A gardener is asked about a tree. Was it planted, or did it grow on its own. He answers. And he has told you how the tree came to be. He has told you nothing about what the tree is.

Grew on its own is unbegotten. Planted is begotten. Both answers are true. Both leave the nature untouched. From-no-one, begotten, proceeding. Three gardener's answers. Not one of them says what God is.

This is the thing I finally saw. Unbegotten was never a name for the essence. Eunomius put it in the wrong drawer and lost the Son doing it. It is a mode of existence. The Father exists in the mode of being from no one. He did not come to be that. There is no coming in God. He is, that way, forever.

And what makes the Father a person is not the negation, not the from-no-one. It is the positive relation. He is the source. He begets and he breathes. The Son is Son by being from. The Spirit is Spirit by proceeding. Three whos, one what, and the whos are pure relation.

## The formula to keep

The Spirit has his existence from the Father. He rests in the Son. He shines through the Son to the world.

Cause on one side. Manifestation on the other. The resting is the hinge between them.

*Anapauetai.* He reposes on the Son. It is the dove at the Jordan that came down and remained. John 1:32. The Fathers read that remaining as an eternal fact and not just a morning on the river.

Keep the resting and the shining in the manifestation drawer and every Orthodox nods. Let them leak into the cause and you are back in the war.

From the Father. Resting in the Son. Shining through the Son. That is the sentence. It is right, and it is enough.
